How to compare pet insurance policies

Comparing pet insurance is mostly an exercise in reading the fine print. The headline premium tells you what you pay each month. It does not tell you what you get back, how long you wait before cover starts, or which conditions are excluded for your breed.

Four numbers that decide the value

The first is the reimbursement rate, the percentage of the eligible invoice the insurer pays after the excess. The second is the annual benefit limit, the ceiling on total claims in a year. The third is the excess, the amount you pay before the insurer contributes. The fourth is the per-condition limit, which caps a single illness or injury even when the annual limit is much higher.

A policy with a ninety per cent reimbursement rate and a five hundred dollar excess often beats a cheaper policy with a sixty per cent rate, particularly for surgery. Run the numbers against a realistic vet bill rather than the premium alone.

Waiting periods and exclusions

Illness cover usually starts after thirty days, and cruciate ligament conditions often carry a longer waiting period. Pre-existing conditions are excluded, and insurers differ on how they define one. A condition your vet noted but did not treat may still count.

Breed-specific exclusions are worth checking before you buy. Hip dysplasia in labradors, brachycephalic airway surgery in pugs and skin allergies in staffies are common exclusions or loadings.

Switching policies

If you switch, keep continuous cover. A gap of even a few days can reset waiting periods and turn an existing condition into a pre-existing one. Ask the new insurer to confirm in writing how they will treat anything your previous policy covered.
