Accident-only pet insurance

Accident-only cover pays for treatment after a sudden event: a car accident, a fall, a fight with another animal, a snake bite or a swallowed object. It is the cheapest form of pet insurance and the easiest to understand, because the claim triggers are physical injuries rather than illnesses.

What it typically excludes matters just as much. Cancer, diabetes, arthritis, dental disease, skin allergies and infections are illnesses, not accidents, so they fall outside the policy. If your dog develops a chronic condition at eight years old, accident-only cover will not contribute to the ongoing medication.

Who it suits

Accident-only policies suit owners on a tight budget, indoor cats that rarely leave the house, and older pets where comprehensive cover has become expensive or unavailable. They also work as a stopgap while you save into a dedicated vet fund.

Check the per-condition limit rather than only the annual limit. A cruciate ligament surgery can cost several thousand dollars on its own, and a policy with a generous annual cap but a low per-condition cap will leave you short.

How it compares

Comprehensive accident and illness cover costs more but includes the diagnostics, specialist referrals and long term medication that older animals need. The gap between the two widens as your pet ages, which is why many owners move to accident-only cover later in life rather than cancelling insurance altogether.

If you are weighing the two, compare the reimbursement rate and the excess as well as the premium. A ninety per cent reimbursement rate on a policy with a high excess can still beat a cheaper policy that only returns sixty per cent.
