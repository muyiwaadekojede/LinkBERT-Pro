Choosing a vet for an older dog

As a dog enters its senior years, the relationship with the vet changes from occasional vaccinations to regular monitoring. Blood panels, dental checks and mobility assessments become part of the annual routine, and continuity matters more than a convenient location.

Look for a clinic that runs senior health screens and explains the results in plain language. Ask whether they have ultrasound and digital x-ray on site, because older dogs often need imaging for heart, liver and joint problems. A clinic that refers everything to a specialist adds cost and delay.

Questions worth asking

Ask how the clinic handles emergencies after hours, whether they offer nurse consultations for repeat checks, and how they price long term medication. A practice that dispenses prescriptions with a reasonable markup saves money over a year of monthly tablets.

It also helps to ask how they record conversations. A written treatment plan with costs attached makes it easier to decide what to do next, and easier to claim if you have insurance.
