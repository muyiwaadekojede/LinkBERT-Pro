Pet insurance for senior cats and dogs

Why take out pet insurance for senior cats and dogs? Cats and dogs often require more veterinary care as they grow older, and pet insurance can help by covering a portion of your eligible vet bills. Other benefits of pet insurance for senior pets may include cover for accidental injuries, including car accidents, poisoning, snake and insect bites, and wounds.

Policies for older animals often come with limited benefits for specified illnesses, and age limits apply. Being able to choose your own vet or clinic for treatment matters more as your pet ages, because continuity of care becomes part of the treatment plan. A financial safety net and peace of mind if your senior pet needs emergency care is the main reason owners take out cover at this stage.

Choosing pet insurance for senior pets

If you are looking to buy or switch to a new policy for your older companion, consider the following. Basic policies for older pets often have a lower benefit percentage, so you will have to pay a larger portion of eligible vet bills. For example, you may only be able to claim up to sixty per cent back for eligible treatments, meaning you would have to foot the remaining forty per cent of the bill.

Comprehensive pet insurance is usually available for pets up until they turn nine. If you take out a suitable policy before that age, your dog or cat can be protected for life, provided you maintain continuous cover past that point. Optional routine care is also available for older pets up to nine years of age as an add-on for new policyholders.

Some insurers offer accident-only cover for senior pets before or up to sixteen years of age. A basic level of cover for injuries will be cheaper than more comprehensive accident and illness cover, but it will not pay for cancer treatment, dental disease or the diagnostic imaging that older pets frequently need.

What to check before switching

Pre-existing conditions are almost never covered, and any gap in cover can turn a manageable condition into an excluded one. Ask the insurer how they define a pre-existing condition, how long the waiting period is for illness claims, and whether the premium rises with age.
