Routine care add-ons

Routine care is an optional add-on that pays a fixed amount toward preventive treatment: annual vaccinations, desexing, microchipping, dental cleaning, flea and tick prevention and a yearly health check. It sits on top of an accident or comprehensive policy rather than replacing it.

The economics are simple and slightly uncomfortable. Most add-ons cost between eighty and two hundred dollars a year and return a similar amount in benefits, spread across several sub-limits. You are pre-paying for care you would probably buy anyway, with the convenience of a claim instead of an upfront bill.

When it makes sense

Routine care is worth considering if you would struggle to pay for desexing or a dental scale and polish in one go, or if a fixed monthly cost helps you budget. It is less useful if you already set aside money for preventive care, because the sub-limits rarely cover the full invoice.

Check whether the add-on requires the main policy to stay active for a minimum period, and whether unused benefits roll over. Most insurers reset the balance each year, so an unused dental benefit disappears.

What it does not cover

Routine care does not cover treatment for illness or injury, and it does not raise the annual benefit limit of the underlying policy. If your dog needs surgery for a swallowed toy, that claim goes through the accident section, not the routine care add-on.
